Privacy

In short: this app collects nothing about you. No account, no ads and no tracking. The maker of the app can’t see what you look up. Only when you say you ate something does an anonymous signal go to the shared counter.

What stays on your phone

Everything you put in the app, and your settings. It’s only stored in the browser on your own phone. Delete the app or the website data and it’s gone. That’s why you can make a backup; you keep that file yourself, wherever you like.

Services that see something

Like any website, the services the app uses see your IP address:

  • GitHub Pages delivers the app itself.
  • Cloudflare keeps the shared counter. When you choose “Eaten”, it only receives the estimated weight (for example 500 grams) and a random number your phone makes up itself. Not which product, not when you checked it, and nothing about you. That number only exists to stop anyone from inflating the counter (20 per day at most) and is deleted after 2 days.
  • Stripe processes the payment when you support Datechecker or buy Plus. Stripe then sees your payment details and email address, like with any online payment. Datechecker itself doesn’t get them: after payment, the counter only checks with Stripe whether payment was made, and stores an unlock code. Who paid isn’t stored with it.

The fonts are built into the app itself; no other service is used for them.

Delete everything

Remove the app from your home screen, or clear the website data in your Safari settings.

Two kinds of dates

Best before

Best before

A quality date. Until that date the food is at its best. After it, it’s often still fine: look, smell and taste. This is where you can save the most food.

For example: bread, yoghurt, cheese, eggs, pasta, rice, biscuits.

Use by

Use by

A safety date. Don’t eat it after the date, even if it looks fine. You can’t see or smell harmful bacteria. You can freeze it before the date, though.

For example: fresh meat, chicken, fish, cold cuts.
